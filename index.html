      So how does a wing make lift?

      Turning the air

      A wing at an angle pushes air downward behind it (downwash). Push air down, and the air pushes the wing up. That's Newton's third law.

      Pressure difference

      Air over the curved top speeds up and its pressure drops. Air underneath is slower, with higher pressure. The difference pushes the wing up (Bernoulli).

      Same thing, two views

      These aren't rival theories. They describe the same airflow. More angle means more turning, a bigger pressure difference and more lift, until the wing stalls.

      Registration prefixes — the aircraft's "country code"

      Rules of thumb

      • A first call is "[station you're calling], [your call sign]" — e.g. "Westbrook Tower, G-ZZXY".
      • Light aircraft use their registration, spelled out phonetically. After ATC shortens it first, the pilot may too (G-ZZXY → "Golf X-ray Yankee").
      • Airliners use a telephony designator plus flight number ("AEROLEARN four five one"). The ICAO code (ALW451) is what appears on the radar label.
      • HEAVY or SUPER warns others about strong wake turbulence behind big aircraft.
      • The call sign goes at the end of every readback.

      How far does VHF reach?

      VHF and UHF travel in straight lines, so range depends on the height of both antennas (the radio horizon). Approximate formula: range (NM) ≈ 1.23 × (√aircraft height ft + √ground antenna height ft). Terrain and transmitter power can reduce it.
